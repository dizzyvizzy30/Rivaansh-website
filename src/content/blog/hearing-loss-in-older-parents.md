Age-related hearing loss comes on gradually, often over years. Many people adjust without realising, so family members are usually the first to notice.

## Signs to look out for

- The TV or phone volume is louder than everyone else likes
- Asking people to repeat themselves, or answering the wrong question
- Difficulty following conversation in groups, at functions or on the phone
- Withdrawing from conversations or social events
- Ringing or buzzing in the ears

## What a hearing test involves

The doctor first examines the ears to check for wax or infection, which are easy to treat. A hearing test then measures how well each ear hears different sounds. It is painless and takes a short time.

## If a hearing aid is advised

- Hearing aids are fitted and adjusted using the hearing test results.
- It usually takes a few weeks of daily use to get used to them — at first, ordinary sounds may seem loud.
- Follow-up visits help fine-tune them.

## How to help at home

- Face the person when you speak, so they can see your lips.
- Speak clearly and a little slower — shouting does not help.
- Turn off the TV or move away from noise when you talk.

Treating hearing loss early helps people stay part of family conversations and social life.
