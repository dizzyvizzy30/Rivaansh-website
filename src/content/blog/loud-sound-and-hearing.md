Very loud sound damages the tiny hair cells in the inner ear that turn sound into signals for the brain. These cells do not grow back, so the damage can be permanent.

## Warning signs after a loud event

- Ringing or buzzing in the ears
- Sounds seeming muffled
- Needing to turn the volume up more than usual

These often fade within a day, but repeated exposure can cause lasting hearing loss.

## At garba, weddings and DJ events

- Stay well away from the speakers.
- Take breaks in a quieter place.
- Use foam earplugs — they reduce loudness but still let you hear music and conversation.
- Keep young children and babies away from loud speakers.

## With earphones

- Follow the **60/60 rule**: no more than 60% volume, for no more than 60 minutes at a time.
- If someone next to you can hear your music, it is too loud.
- Noise-cancelling earphones help you listen at lower volume in noisy places.

## When to see a doctor

See a doctor if ringing or muffled hearing lasts more than a day. **Sudden hearing loss in one ear should be seen within 24 hours.**
