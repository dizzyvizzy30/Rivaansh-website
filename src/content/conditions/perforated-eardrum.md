## What it is

The eardrum is a thin membrane that vibrates with sound. A hole in it can be caused by:

- A middle-ear infection, when pus builds up behind the eardrum
- An injury - a slap on the ear, a cotton bud, hairpin or other object pushed into the ear
- A sudden change in pressure, such as a loud blast, diving or flying
- Long-standing ear infections

## Common symptoms

- Discharge from the ear
- Reduced hearing in that ear
- Ear pain, sometimes followed by sudden relief when the ear starts to discharge
- Ringing in the ear

## When to see a doctor

See an ENT doctor if you think your eardrum may be damaged, or if an ear discharges or hearing is reduced.

Until then, **keep the ear dry** (a cotton-wool ball with a little petroleum jelly in the outer ear while bathing), and do not put oil or drops in the ear unless a doctor has prescribed them.

### When it's urgent

Seek urgent care if an ear injury is followed by **severe dizziness or vomiting, weakness of the face, high fever, or swelling behind the ear**.

## What happens at your visit

- Examination of the eardrum with an otoscope or microscope
- A hearing test
- Sometimes a swab of the discharge to choose the right treatment

## Treatment options

- **Waiting and keeping the ear dry** - many small holes caused by injury heal within six to eight weeks.
- **Ear drops or antibiotics** to treat infection.

### Eardrum repair surgery

If the hole does not heal, or the ear keeps discharging, the eardrum can be repaired with an operation called **tympanoplasty** or **myringoplasty**. A small piece of the patient's own tissue is used to close the hole. The operation is usually planned when the ear is dry. Closing the hole helps keep infection out and can improve hearing.

## If you need a procedure

See [Before & After Surgery](/pages/care.html) for general guidance. You will be given instructions for your specific operation.

## Questions patients ask

### Will my hearing come back?
Hearing usually improves when the hole heals or is repaired. The hearing test helps show how much improvement to expect.

### Can I bathe and wash my hair?
Yes - keep water out of the ear as described above.

### Can I fly?
Ask the doctor before flying, especially after surgery.
