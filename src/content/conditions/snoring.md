## What it is

Snoring happens when the airway narrows during sleep. Common contributors are:

- A blocked nose (allergy, a bent septum, polyps)
- Large tonsils or adenoids - the most common cause in children
- Extra weight, alcohol or sleeping tablets in the evening, smoking, and sleeping on your back

**Obstructive sleep apnoea (OSA)** is when the airway repeatedly closes during sleep, causing pauses in breathing.

## Common symptoms

- Loud snoring most nights
- Pauses in breathing, gasping or choking during sleep (often noticed by a family member)
- Waking up tired, morning headaches, sleepiness during the day
- In children: snoring most nights, restless sleep, mouth breathing, bed-wetting, and daytime tiredness or poor attention

## When to see a doctor

See an ENT doctor if snoring comes with pauses in breathing or daytime sleepiness, or if a child snores most nights.

### When it's urgent

If you **fall asleep while driving** or at work, stop driving and see a doctor promptly.

## What happens at your visit

- Examination of the nose, mouth and throat, including nasal endoscopy
- Questions about sleep, tiredness and health conditions such as high blood pressure
- A **sleep study** when sleep apnoea is suspected

## Treatment options

- **Lifestyle changes** - weight loss, avoiding alcohol in the evening, sleeping on your side, stopping smoking.
- **Treating a blocked nose** with sprays or surgery.
- **CPAP**, a machine that keeps the airway open during sleep, for moderate or severe sleep apnoea.
- **Surgery in selected cases** - for example removing large tonsils and adenoids in children, or operations on the nose or throat in adults.

## Questions patients ask

### Is snoring harmful?
Snoring on its own is mostly a nuisance. Snoring with sleep apnoea can affect your health and should be assessed.

### Will surgery stop my snoring?
It depends on the cause. The examination shows whether surgery is likely to help.
