## What it is

- **Tonsillitis** is an infection of the tonsils, causing a sore throat, fever and painful swallowing. Most cases are caused by viruses.
- **Quinsy** is a collection of pus beside a tonsil, usually on one side.
- **Large tonsils and adenoids** in children can cause snoring, mouth breathing, pauses in breathing during sleep, a constantly blocked nose and repeated ear problems.

## Common symptoms

- Sore throat and pain on swallowing, with fever
- Bad breath, swollen glands in the neck
- In children: loud snoring, sleeping with the mouth open, restless sleep, a nasal voice, or repeated ear infections

## When to see a doctor

See an ENT doctor for sore throats that keep coming back, a sore throat that does not improve after a week, or a child who snores most nights or stops breathing briefly in sleep.

### When it's urgent

Seek urgent care for **difficulty breathing, drooling or being unable to swallow saliva, difficulty opening the mouth, or one-sided throat swelling with a muffled voice** - and for **any bleeding after tonsil surgery**.

## What happens at your visit

- Examination of the throat, neck and ears
- A look at the adenoids with a thin camera through the nose (endoscopy), or sometimes an X-ray
- A hearing test if there are ear problems

## Treatment options

- **Fluids, rest and pain relief** for viral sore throats.
- **Antibiotics** when a bacterial infection is likely.
- **Drainage** of a quinsy.

### Tonsil and adenoid surgery

Removing the tonsils (**tonsillectomy**) may be advised when severe tonsillitis keeps returning, or when large tonsils block breathing during sleep. Removing the adenoids (**adenoidectomy**) may help a child with a blocked nose, snoring or repeated ear problems. The operations are done under general anaesthesia. Recovery usually takes about 10–14 days, with soft food, plenty of fluids and regular pain relief.

## Questions patients ask

### Does removing tonsils weaken immunity?
The body has many other parts that fight infection, and removing the tonsils does not leave a child unprotected.

### Is there a best age for surgery?
There is no fixed age. The decision depends on how often problems occur and how much they affect breathing, sleep and school.

### What can my child eat after the operation?
Soft, cool foods and plenty of fluids at first. Eating normally as soon as possible helps healing - follow the instructions you are given.
